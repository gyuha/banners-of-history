Historical Background
Date & Era

207 BCE · End of Qin · Anti-Qin rebellion
As Qin besieged Zhao at Julu, Xiang Yu crossed the river to attack the siege army and its supply network.

The Opposing Armies

Xiang Yu’s Chu army aided the besieged Zhao force while other rebel armies initially watched. Wang Li commanded Qin’s siege troops. Zhang Han led a separate field force supporting them through a supply corridor; these were not one camp garrison.

Why They Fought

After defeating Xiang Liang at Dingtao, Qin advanced north and trapped King Xie of Zhao and Zhang Er at Julu. Other relief armies feared Qin and hesitated. Sent to relieve Zhao, Xiang Yu took command after killing Song Yi and decided on a river crossing and decisive attack.

The Battle

In 207 BCE Xiang Yu sent an advance force across, then committed his army, destroying cooking vessels and boats and carrying short-term rations. Repeated attacks cut Qin’s supply corridor and defeated Wang Li’s army. Other rebel forces subsequently joined the pursuit.

Historical Outcome

Wang Li was captured and Julu relieved, establishing Xiang Yu’s military prestige. Zhang Han was not immediately killed here; he surrendered during later fighting and political pressure. Cutting supplies, defeating the siege army and securing Zhang Han’s surrender were related but distinct stages.

Recommended Side · Chu Army
Commander: 10,000 HP and 60% damage reduction, accompanied by a small elite guard. Health recovers out of combat.
Tactics
Your commander and picked Chu troops can break dense formations. Cut off the two supply camps first.
Dash through massed Qin troops and use area attacks to open a path for your infantry.
Qin’s main camp is on high ground. Watch for crossbows and reinforcements on the flank.
