Historical Background
Date & Era

1066 CE · Medieval England · End of Anglo-Saxon rule and the Norman Conquest
A disputed succession brought Harold II and William of Normandy into a struggle for the English crown.

The Opposing Armies

King Harold II defended the ridge with household warriors and levies fighting on foot. Duke William brought Norman, Breton and other contingents, combining archers, infantry and cavalry.

Why They Fought

Edward the Confessor died in 1066 without a direct heir. English nobles chose Harold, while William claimed that the succession had been promised to him and assembled an invasion fleet. Harold defeated a Norwegian invasion in the north, then marched south to face the Normans.

The Battle

On 14 October the English held the heights with shields and spears, while elite warriors fought with axes. Norman archers, infantry and cavalry attacked repeatedly. Some English troops pursued retreating enemies away from their formation and high ground, exposing themselves to counterattack. Fighting continued into the evening.

Historical Outcome

Harold was killed and the English line collapsed. William later entered London and was crowned king. Harold’s precise manner of death and some tactical details remain disputed. Formation discipline and the combined protection of spears, shields and terrain are central to the battle.

Tactics
Hold four spear companies facing west in front of the north and south flags. Use Hold (X) to brace; moving or pursuing lowers their spears.
Shields absorb arrows. Keep axemen behind the spear line and archers on the reverse slope. Turn nearby pikes to meet cavalry on the flanks.
Secure one flag first. Avoid chasing cavalry downhill with the entire army; select individual companies and leave a reserve at the other flag.
